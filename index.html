The domestic dog (Canis lupus familiaris) presents the field naturalist with a subject of inexhaustible fascination. In over four decades of careful observation, this correspondent has yet to encounter a specimen that failed to be, in some fundamental and measurable respect, a Good Boy. The evidence is, at this point, overwhelming.

Distributed across six continents, the dog has adapted to every known environment: arctic tundra, tropical rainforest, suburban sofa, and the passenger seat of a mid-size saloon. In each habitat, it performs the same essential behaviours with the same essential enthusiasm, and expects, in return, only that you notice.

"No other species has, through sheer force of personality, convinced an entire genus of apex predators to carry small bags on its behalf."

This edition concerns itself with the six primary field types encountered most frequently in British and European specimens, each catalogued with the rigour appropriate to a subject of this magnitude.

A Note on Methodology

All observations herein were conducted at close range, often at ground level. Several resulted in the observer being sat upon. This is considered standard procedure.

Regarding the Tail

The tail is a reliable indicator of internal state. A wagging tail does not, however, preclude the dog from also being Wrong About the Postman. These are not mutually exclusive conditions.

Retraction (Vol. XLVI)

We previously reported that Labradors "will eventually stop eating." We retract this claim without reservation. We were mistaken. There is no evidence.

Field Reports — Primary Specimens

Specimen 001  ·  Highland Origin

Golden Retriever

Canis lupus familiaris aureus

First observed in the Scottish Highlands, circa 1868, fetching things. It has not stopped since. The specimen exhibits a constitutional inability to distinguish between objects that require fetching and objects that do not. The distinction, it has decided, is irrelevant.

Retrieval Enthusiasm Wet Fur Good Boy

Classified Field Observation

"Has been observed fetching the same stick from the same pond for a continuous period of six hours. When asked why, it fetched the stick again. The question was answered."

Specimen 002  ·  German Origin

German Shepherd

Canis lupus familiaris germanicus

A specimen of considerable gravity. Approaches all situations with the bearing of a senior detective who has seen things. Is, in fact, primarily concerned with whether you have arrived home at the expected time. You have not. An explanation will be required.

Vigilance Loyalty Mild Disappointment

Classified Field Observation

"Subject maintained unbroken eye contact for fourteen minutes following observer's return home at 6:43pm rather than the customary 6:30pm. An apology was eventually offered. The subject accepted it with visible reservations."

Specimen 003  ·  French Origin

French Bulldog

Canis lupus familiaris gallicus compactus

Aerodynamically improbable. Snores with the authority of a much larger animal. Despite a face suggesting permanent existential crisis, the specimen maintains an overall disposition of deep satisfaction. It has considered its options and has chosen the sofa.

Compact Stubborn Loudly Asleep

Classified Field Observation

"When presented with a flight of stairs, the subject sat at the bottom and looked upward for an extended period. It then looked at the observer. The observer carried the subject upstairs. Both parties found this arrangement satisfactory."

Specimen 004  ·  British Origin

Border Collie

Canis lupus familiaris britannus velocissimus

Operates at a frequency slightly above what the human nervous system can comfortably process. Has already completed the task you were about to assign it. Is now looking at you with an expression that can only be described as politely urgent. There is more work to be done.

Velocity Herding More Herding Eyes

Classified Field Observation

"Subject was observed herding a group of pigeons in St James's Park. The pigeons were not cooperative. The subject revised its strategy eleven times in four minutes. On the twelfth attempt, the pigeons moved slightly to the left. Subject appeared satisfied."

Specimen 005  ·  English Origin

Labrador Retriever

Canis lupus familiaris edax perpetuus

The Labrador represents, in this correspondent's view, the apex of unbridled optimism in the animal kingdom. Every walk may contain a sandwich. Every stranger may have a biscuit. Every bin may yield treasure. So far, none of these hypotheses has been definitively disproved.

Hunger More Hunger Good Boy Food?

Classified Field Observation

"Subject consumed, in a single morning: one full breakfast, a child's dropped ice cream, an unattended sausage roll, and what appeared to be a substantial portion of a hedge. Subject showed no signs of being full. Subject is never full. There is no bottom."

Specimen 006  ·  Irish Origin

Irish Setter

Canis lupus familiaris hibernicus magnificus

Widely regarded as the most aesthetically spectacular of the domestic field types. The specimen is aware of this. It moves through open countryside with the manner of something that expects to be photographed, and is usually correct. The hair is not accidental. Nothing about the Irish Setter is accidental.

Spectacular Self-Aware Mahogany

Classified Field Observation

"Subject paused at the crest of a hill into a headwind for approximately forty seconds before continuing. When asked if it had done this deliberately, it did not answer. It did not need to answer. We both knew."

Field Dispatches

On the Subject of Squirrels

After forty-seven years of pursuit, no domestic dog has successfully caught a squirrel in this correspondent's observation area. The squirrels are aware of this. The dogs are not.

Regarding the Cone of Shame

The cone does not diminish the dog's fundamental dignity. The dog is not aware it has diminished its dignity. The dog's dignity is, in this respect, indestructible.

The 3am Bark

Something was out there. We may never know what. The dog knows. The dog has decided not to elaborate.

On Walks as a Concept

The dog understands the walk as a series of incredibly important smells connected by short stretches of walking. The human's version of events differs considerably.
